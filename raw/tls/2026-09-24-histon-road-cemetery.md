# 正文

I confess that I am what my (long dead) mum used to call a “churchyard creeper”. To put it another way, some of my favourite quiet places in the world are graveyards, from the manicured and well-mown to the brambly and overgrown.

There’s the [“non-Catholic” cemetery](https://cemeteryrome.it/en/homepage-eng/) in Rome, for example, the last resting place of [John Keats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Keats) and [Antonio Gramsci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Gramsci) among hundreds of others. In Crete, I have a fondness for the [Souda Bay Commonwealth War Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souda_Bay_War_Cemetery), its almost aggressive neatness being mitigated by the glorious location overlooking the Aegean. It’s the final home of some 1,500 soldiers, most killed in the [Battle of Crete](https://www.cwgc.org/our-work/blog/remembering-the-battle-of-crete/) during the Second World War. (My go-to grave there is that of [John Pendlebury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pendlebury), the cutting-edge archaeologist executed by German soldiers in 1941.)

In Cambridge, there is the wonderful [Ascension Parish Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground), which has the graves of a great selection of famous academics of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries (including [Richard Jebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Claverhouse_Jebb), [J. G. Frazer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_George_Frazer) and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein)). But it is the [Histon Road cemetery](https://histonroadcemetery.org/) – established in 1843 “for people of all religious persuasions” – that has become my all-time favourite. In fact, when I did a “Cambridge walk” feature for the _Observer_ in the spring (it’s [here](https://observer.co.uk/news/observer-walk/article/mary-beard-the-observer-walk?utm_campaign=601711_20260711+Observer+Daily+Weekend&utm_medium=email&utm_source=dotdigital), paywalled), I chose to avoid the dreaming spires and instead to start out from Histon Road and end up at Ascension. My enthusiasm must have spilled over, as the [Friends of Histon Road Cemetery](https://histonroadcemetery.org/friends/) (now in their twentieth year) then asked me to be their patron. I accepted with delight.

I’m partly attached to the place because it has been almost in my backyard for decades. For most of the fifty or so years that I have lived in Cambridge, I have been within about fifteen minutes’ walk from it. It’s also been a very useful short cut between two main roads and between some excellent pubs.

But short…
