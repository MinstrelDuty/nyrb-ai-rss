# 正文

In September 1972, Seamus Heaney moved from Belfast to Wicklow in the Republic of Ireland, a turning point in his life he writes about in “Exposure”, the final poem in _North_ (1975). In the face of the expectation that Heaney take a more explicit political position in the fierce sectarian conflict in Northern Ireland, “Exposure” emphasizes the importance, for his poetry, of not listening “to what is said behind-backs”. Although the voices of the rain through the trees “mutter about let-downs and erosions”, every drop recalls “the diamond absolutes”: he is “neither internee nor informer”.

In Justin Quinn’s poem “Therapy”, first published in the _TLS_ in 2014 and then in _Early House_ (2015), Heaney is the one who, “when sick of people’s havering … walked and walked till he / had calmed this mood of his / and heard the earth again” – a writer able to find confirmation and strength in nature. But Quinn’s attempts to deal with his grief at Heaney’s death by imitating the older poet’s coping mechanisms (“I went straight out the door / and walked”) fail in his own semi-urban surroundings, scarred by “foil / and plastic in the soil / along the path”. This is a landscape in which Quinn’s pastoral archaisms – his “meadows, fields and hedges” – feel out of place, proving that any attempt to summon the dead by melting into their version of the world is fundamentally misguided.

In his _Cambridge Introduction to Modern Irish Poetry_(2008), Quinn considers how subsequent generations of Irish poets might have to navigate Heaney’s massive legacy. Where Heaney moved past Yeats’s soaring mythologies by digging down into the earth, his successors may need to leave the soil behind entirely, uprooting themselves artistically and even physically. Quinn himself lives in Prague among post-Communist concrete tower blocks and translates Slavic poetry. Like other Irish poets, he can only move forward when he stops trying to _be_ Heaney; the real therapy might be writing a poem about that.

**Therapy**

till gradually the land  
lay clean beneath the skies.
