# 正文

Everybody seems to be scamming everybody else in Randy Boyagoda’s _Lords of Serendipity_. Allard, a teaching assistant at the fictional Arcadia Tech in the US, sells grades to struggling international students. For a “reasonable” fee, he sneaks into the system and “fixes” his clients’ results. His services are in such demand that he soon becomes a millionaire. “So far, he’d made $2.25 million. He needed to raise another $1.75 million.” The reason he requires all this money is to fund his own “endowed chair” in statistics. It has long been Allard’s conviction that this will be the best way to attract the attention of his father, Martin, himself “an endowed research chair, in astronomy, at a well-known university in the greater Boston area. Who had never acknowledged his existence”. The last time Martin spoke with Allard’s mother was more than twenty years previously. Having conned her into sleeping with him, Martin – always the gentleman – kindly offered to give her a ride to the nearest abortion clinic.

One of the clients of Allard’s grade- fixing services is an Indian student, Adi Bhopal. When he is not being scammed by Allard, Adi runs his own scheming business. While pretending to be studying at a leading English university, he is “the sole proprietor of an exclusive international tutoring company”, operated via WhatsApp and dedicated to helping young people desperate to study abroad to part with their families’ money. The sole client and victim of Adi’s con operation is Devi, a high-schooler from Sri Lanka. Fortunately for Devi, she has some wiles of her own, and Martin, as it happens, is in Sri Lanka. When their paths collide, not only does Martin take notice of Devi, he seems singularly receptive to her conning charms. Her tale of oppression and struggle in “the global South”, he realizes, is exactly the kind of story his PC-obsessed institution needs for its marketing campaigns. Having lost the funding for his chair, he now oversees a research centre at Serendipity University (Serendip was Sri Lanka’s name in Persian), established through a complicated funding scheme via China, which never misses an opportunity to scam America out of its leading talent. And talent is something Martin has in abundance – particularly for doctoring statistical data and conning his peers into believing that he is on the cusp of a major astronomical discovery.
