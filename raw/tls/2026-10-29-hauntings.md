# 正文

“Dear Steve, Sometime last year I bought an evocative, rather haunted-feeling book called REDHEADED PECKERWOOD.”

So began an email that landed in Stephen King’s inbox in late October 2013. The sender was Peter Straub – after King, perhaps the foremost horror novelist of the past fifty years. Straub’s novels, which include _Ghost Story_ (1979) and _Mr. X_ (1999), deftly combine genre tropes with poetic invention.

“What if”, Straub emailed King, “a series of motiveless, brutal crimes had been committed in the Far West of the Territories?” The perpetrators are a young couple, and Straub based them on the subjects of the “haunted-feeling book”, Christian Patterson’s _Redheaded Peckerwood_ (2010). Charles Starkweather and Caril Ann Fugate were American teenagers who went on a killing spree in 1958. In Straub’s retelling, Sawyer, an ex-cop, would investigate these murders, ultimately crossing back to his home world.

King liked the idea, but was busy with other projects. “I knew it had to be written”, he confides in an afterword to _Other Worlds Than These_, “but I thought … there will be time.” There was not: Straub died in 2022. Working off his notes, King took it upon himself to fashion an epic finale in his friend’s memory. He has accomplished it. “I channelled Peter like a mad mother”, King writes. It is true that some sections of the book read like Straub: the prose is flowery and revels in its own lyricism. But ultimately this still feels more like a King novel, with the author fully leaning into his own lore. (As was hinted at in _Black House_, the Territories are actually Mid-World, the setting of King’s expansive _Dark Tower_ series.)

Readers will not need to know…
