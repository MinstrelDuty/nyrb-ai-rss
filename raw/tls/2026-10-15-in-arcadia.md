# In Arcadia

October 16, 2026

[Read this issue](https://www.the-tls.com/issues/current-issue)

Together again, my father and I sit  
through this late afternoon, watching  
the sprinkler turn its arms, a clock  
throwing mist across the lawn.

His breath at eighty-six  
is a small bird, restless in its cage.  
Elbow to elbow on the splintered bench,  
we smile as a tomcat startles a dove

out of the hedge, crickets outgrow  
their shells in the pinned heat.  
The sun stalls in the fretwork  
of plane trees, and here we are,

caught in a grid of light, breathing  
the smoke of spitted Easter lamb  
from his boyhood home.  
Out of the limestone earth,

Alpheios drags dark currents to the mill,  
spinning water, livening the farms.  
My father crossed that river, long ago,  
his shadow splitting on the rocks.

_Vasiliki Albedo’s poems have appeared in_ Poetry London _,_ The Poetry Review _and elsewhere. She has been commended in the National Poetry Competition and shortlisted for a Forward prize for best single poem._
