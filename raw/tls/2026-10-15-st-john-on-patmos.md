# 正文

The Australian poet Peter Porter thought those who found the King James Bible’s language too complicated to communicate theological truths, and who insisted on “ironing out the wrinkles”, were making a terrible mistake: “You can’t do it. Theology is a wrinkly business and you have to absorb it through the best minds that took it on”. In his poem “St. John on Patmos”, first published in the _TLS_ in 1965 and then in _A Porter Folio_ (1969), he imagines the author of the Book of Revelation recording his extraordinary visions on Patmos, the Greek island to which he was exiled by the Roman Emperor Domitian. Not conventionally religious, Porter regarded Christianity as a vast imaginative and historical canvas rather than a set of absolute spiritual truths. In ‘“St. John…”there is a sense of him reframing John’s visions as the hallucinations of a feverish old man, less in need of God’s grace than of medical help. Porter appears interested in the way religious imagination overlaps with human frailty and need: John’s attempts to fortify the persecuted members of the early church derive some of their power, perhaps, from the hardships he was also having to endure.

But his creative struggles are also part of Porter’s meaning. His speaker is an artist looking for a solution to a technical problem: “For the right visions / You need a desert or an island”. As the artistic idea begins to clarify, the voice becomes more recognizably that of the poet himself, excitedly pursuing the growth of his own poem: “The missed heart beat / Reforms a whole landscape”. It may begin in sublimity and rapture, but it can only come to life with “the world’s help … Sure verbs to bind the vision to the rock”.

**St. John on Patmos**

For the right visions  
You need a desert or an island.

On an island the beasts will listen,  
The groundsel sneak into your cell,

Evil done on the mainland  
Will let down a bloody feather at your door,

You’ll get a lion to boss your bestiary  
And the best menagerie of all in your head.

What a ramshackle day! The missed heart beat  
Reforms a whole landscape:

That sail is bringing pilgrims, a hymn to God  
In your convalescence – the passion-fruit flower
