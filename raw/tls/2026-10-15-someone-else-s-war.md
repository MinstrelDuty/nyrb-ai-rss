# 正文

Patrick deWitt’s novels rove freely over history, geography and genre. His debut, _Ablutions_ (2009), was a Charles Bukowski-worthy late-1970s-ish study of a Los Angeles barfly; _The Sisters Brothers_ (2011) was a gold rush-era western. Next came a nineteenth-century Mitteleuropean fairy tale, _Undermajordomo Minor_ (2015), then a chronicling of the downfall of a caustic Manhattan socialite (_French Exit_, 2018) and, in _The Librarianist_(2023), a moving portrait of a placid Portland librarian. Nevertheless, deWitt’s knack for sharp and memorable characterization, and an enchanting ability to make even the most murderous, self-indulgent or selfless central character seem likeable, unite the works. These are sure-handed and intelligent novels, and deWitt’s latest, _Dodge City_, is no exception.

The Dodge City of the title has nothing to do with the notorious Wild West outpost. It is the late 1960s and the novel’s hero, Lee Clarke, has been kicked out of UCLA following a fistfight; his suddenly unenrolled status voids his deferment, summoning forth the draft notice that drops through his letterbox. Lee has a desirable girlfriend, Cal, and a disinclination to go to war. In one of the book’s many heady, humorous conversations, a draft officer dismisses Lee’s concern about the sending of someone (him) to a war he “really” doesn’t want to go to, stating: “This is not uncommon, and is one of many wrinkles ironed out across the course of basic training”.

Uninterested in being ironed out, Lee embarks on an amphetamine-fuelled cross-country trip to visit family members, after which he intends to hit the border. With two weeks to go before his army physical, he will have time to see his father, mother, sister and brother – each by turns eccentric, in crisis, or both. Self-discovery is a necessary aspect of any road novel, and each of Lee’s stops leads to some of that, but above all the journey must be episodically engaging, and deWitt keeps the laughs coming and the interactions freighted.

First comes Lee’s father, who has lost his long-term job and is now going through the motions as a dogcatcher in Concrete, Washington, where he drinks nonstop and snatches up a mutt or two per day to meet the daily quota. A Second World War vet, he disapproves of Lee’s draft dodging, sending his son away with the advice: “The war they give you, that’s your war”.

Lee hasn’t seen his mother…
