# 正文

“My dear Edith”, wrote T. S. Eliot in August 1944, “This book shows you different from yourself in earlier work, but still more, always identifiably different from everybody else.” He was writing to thank Edith Sitwell for an inscribed copy of her earthy and regenerative wartime publication _Green Song_(1944). Broad and sweeping in its explorations of spirituality and the depths of human suffering, _Green Song_ was a clear departure from her earlier work, which had been more concerned with language’s capacity for abstraction and play. It was to be her last poetry collection.

“Anne Boleyn’s Song”, first published in the _TLS_ in 1943, especially impressed Eliot, who said he had “been struck by [it]” when he first heard Sitwell read it. The significance of voice endures throughout – that Eliot heard Sitwell, that it is the song of Anne Boleyn, a song which “answered the bird call” and the dirge of Henry, who “sang to my blood”. Sitwell draws attention to the lungs and throat: to a “heedless, headless” speaker who can no longer speak. Separately, her Boleyn notes her casting as a temptress Eve: “Men said I was the primal Fall – / That I gave him the world of spring and youth like an apple”. What she thinks of such a charge she does not say, only that “Henry thought me winter-cold”.

In her descriptions of fecundity and fate, however, Sitwell draws a subtle parallel to the story of another fall: Sitwell’s “bird, the Angel of the Annunciation / Broods over his heaven of wings” owes much to the God of Milton’s _Paradise Lost_, who “Dove-like satst brooding on the vast Abyss”. From her own abyss, “black and deep / As the prenatal sleep”, Sitwell’s Boleyn also possesses the power of creation: Henry watches “While a new world grows in your side”. Yet in his longing for a son, he cannot recognize the world to come – a new, Elizabethan age. His wife, whose wonder he does not see, “turned from him as the world / Turns from the sun … And then the world grew old”.

**Anne Boleyn’s Song**

“After the terrible rain, the Annunciation”  
The bird-blood in the veins that has changed to emeralds  
Answered the bird-call …  
In the neoteric spring the winter coldness  
Will be forgotten  
As I forget the coldness of my last lover,

The great grey King  
Who lies upon my…
