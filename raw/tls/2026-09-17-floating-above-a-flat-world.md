# 正文

There is a sense in _Sail Away Land_, Ben Pester’s third book and second collection of short stories, that all his characters are floating just above the ground. They are pathologically passive men, mired in a flat world that exploits them at every turn. This apathy disconnects them from their bodies and those around them, leaving a state of dissociation. Unable to assert any of their own desires, they let themselves be carried away by the whims of strangers and the numbing pull of television. It’s the concrete specificity of the details and the idiosyncratic whimsy, however, that mark some of Pester’s most distinctive stories.

One is “A goshawk”, which details an epidemic of unmoored stepfathers showing up at various families’ doorsteps. The protagonist is bewildered by this phenomenon, especially since he no longer has any father figure of his own. Instead, he is forced to watch all his friends cancel plans in favour of hanging out with their new stepfathers, feeling like an outsider looking into warmly lit homes and yearning for this strange new flavour of familial intimacy.

“You and me, and Russel Palomet”, on the other hand, has a nightmarish atmosphere, reminiscent of the fairy-tale experience of taking an erroneous path. The narrator is on his way to a holiday with his lover when he is waylaid by a stranger asking for a quick favour. His acquiescence is almost immediate. The willingness to sacrifice – time, convenience, health and happiness – is nothing short of self-sabotage, and he recognizes it even as it happens. “You can lead me by the hand into a prison, and I won’t ask for a penny.”

There are moments when this abject resistance to making choices can grate, notably when narrators question why they’re addressing the audience at all: “What is this for god’s sake? This talking. This talking I’m doing now”. Regardless of how naturally self-critical a character might be, this repeated self-deprecation can make the reader lose confidence in the writer by extension. Other stories can feel like scraps of notepaper that never quite cohered into a whole. The overall experience, however, is one of rare evanescence.
