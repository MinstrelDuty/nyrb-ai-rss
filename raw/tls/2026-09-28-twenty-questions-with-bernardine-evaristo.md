# 正文

[_**Writers and thinkers take on twenty questions from the**_**TLS**_**, revealing their favourite books, writing habits and best advice**_](https://www.the-tls.com/article-section/twenty-questions/)

**What is your favourite book published in the past twelve months?**

**So many to choose from. I guess****Mira Jacob’s****_Good Talk_, a graphic “memoir in conversations”, is high up on my list. Conversations around intersectionality, marriage, politics and family are refracted through an Indian-Jewish American family. It’s totally unique, thought-provoking, often funny, touching and beautifully honest.**

**What subject have you found it most challenging to write about?**

**The English countryside, strange as it seems. I am more comfortable writing about other countries or imagined landscapes than trying to depict rural Britain. I’ve created black women farmers in my new novel, who live in Northumberland, and I really didn’t know where to begin in trying to describe life on a farm. It took a lot of research. I guess the truth is that I haven’t spent much time in the English countryside.**

**Which author (living or dead) do you think is most underrated?**

**[Buchi Emecheta](https://www.the-tls.com/articles/private/from-ptahhotep-to-postcolonialism-8/) (1944–2017), the Nigerian novelist who arrived in the UK in 1962 and subsequently published twenty books. She was one of the original Granta Best of Young British Novelists in 1983 but for the last thirty years of her life she was ignored by the literary establishment. She was the foremother of black British women’s writing and her early books, in particular, were powerful fictions written from and about our lives.**

**Which author (living or dead) do you think is most overrated?**

**I’ll pass on this.**

**What’s the best advice you’ve ever received?**

**To commit to seemingly unachievable goals, work towards them, and then never give up on them, even when they prove to be so unattainable.**

**To what extent, in your view, is writing a political act?**

**All of life is political and so too is writing, intentional, conscious or otherwise. The politics of writing emerges through the choices the writer makes, the messages that are received and how the work might be contextualised and interpreted.**

**Do you have any writing tics?**

**Too many to mention. The word “but” is perhaps the most annoying. I have to do a “but” edit on my books just before they are proofed.**

**What is the first thing you wrote?**

**A poem about the suffragettes when I was about fourteen, which made the school magazine. I still have it somewhere. I don’t remember being a feminist at that age but clearly the suffragettes caught my imagination.**

**How, in your opinion, should we measure a book’s success?**

**Wow, big question. It depends on who is doing the measuring. A writer will have a sense of whether a book has achieved what they set out to do, but this might be contradicted by readers or critics. Books that reach a mass market are commercially successful but this doesn’t necessarily translate to critical success; and vice versa critically celebrated novels might reach a negligible readership. The most successful novels fill me with awe at the author’s creative magic.**

**What do you read on holiday?**

**I don’t really do holidays, but when I take time off I enjoy the novels I’ve been waiting to read for a long time. Reading for pleasure and not for work is too rare an occurrence.**
