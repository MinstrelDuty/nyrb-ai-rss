# 正文

“Sadness of success”, writes Albert Camus in a notebook entry for June 25, 1947: “If everything were more difficult for me, as before, I’d have much more right to say what I say”. The success in question was winning the Prix des Critiques for _La Peste_ (_The Plague)_, published a fortnight earlier. It could be worse, the reader wants to tell him by way of consolation: you could have won the Nobel. Fast-forward ten years and the entry for October 17, 1957, reads: “Nobel. Strange feeling of overwhelming weight and melancholy. At 20 years old, poor and naked, I knew true glory. My mother”.

After a certain age, said Paul Valéry, all that remains are honours. Camus was ambivalent about both age and honours: “At 30 years old”, he writes, “I became famous. I don’t regret it. I might have had bad dreams about it later. Now I know what it amounts to – not much.” One entry is marked simply: “November, 32 years old”. A little later, he adds: “Sad to still feel vulnerable. In 25 years, I’ll be 57. 25 years, then, to do my work and find what I’m looking for … But how far I feel from that excellence I’ll need so much”. In July 1958, he writes: “Before writing a novel, I’ll have to spend years in obscurity”.

These notebooks begin with those years of obscurity, in May 1935, when Camus was a twenty-one-year-old aspiring writer in Oran, Algeria, and end a year and a half before his death in January 1960. The first entry starts, “My point is:”, and it’s a fitting opening to these carnets, in which the voice is never less than direct, frank and self-scrutinizing, even though, as the editor and translator Ryan Bloom reminds us, Camus went over several of the notebooks again when he had them typed up – perhaps in view of publication, certainly in view of posterity. The “point” is one that recurs throughout his writings:

That a person can – without romanticism – feel nostalgic for lost poverty. A certain number of years lived in destitution is enough to develop a sensibility. In this particular case, the curious feeling the son carries for his mother constitutes _his entire sensibility_.
