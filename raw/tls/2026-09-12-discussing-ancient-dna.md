# 正文

A few weeks ago [I posted](https://www.the-tls.com/regular-features/mary-beard-a-dons-life/ancient-dna-blog-post) about an ancient DNA (“aDNA”) exhibition at the [Francis Crick Institute](https://www.crick.ac.uk/) in London, and I mentioned that we would be having a [public discussion](https://www.crick.ac.uk/whats-on/what-can-ancient-dna-tell-us-about-the-romans) in early September about what aDNA can tell us about the Romans. That has now happened, and it will soon be available to watch online.

I chaired the session (taking the role of the non-scientific historian, who needs this kind of stuff clearly explained!), with [Hella Eckhardt](https://www.reading.ac.uk/archaeology/staff/hella-eckardt), [Peter Frankopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Frankopan) and [Marina](https://www.crick.ac.uk/research/find-a-researcher/marina-soares-da-silva)[](https://www.crick.ac.uk/research/find-a-researcher/marina-soares-da-silva)[Soares da Silva](https://www.crick.ac.uk/research/find-a-researcher/marina-soares-da-silva), explaining and debating what difference this kind of analysis is making to Roman history more generally. Encouragingly the occasion was completely booked out, with 400 people there.

We returned to some of the examples that had featured in the exhibition – such as the remains of a second-century [man found buried near the A14](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-67755415) who was proved, by a combination of scientific techniques, to have an ancestry in the Caucasus. And we added a few more tricky cases, including [Beachy Head Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachy_Head_Lady). This is the name given to a skull, discovered near Beachy Head in the 1950s, of a woman who lived (or at least died) in that area, probably around the second century CE.Early analysis of the form of the skull strongly suggested that she had sub-Saharan ancestry, and on that basis, she was hailed as the “first Black Briton”. More recent aDNA analysis indicates that that is incorrect.

I am happy to eat humble pie on this, as I was one of those who used her as an example of diversity in the province, though Hella was generous enough to let me off the hook. She said that when I claimed Beachy Head woman as a person of colour, scientific analysis did indeed indicate exactly that. But science develops, and there is no shame retrospectively in getting it wrong – you do the best you can with the evidence you have.

Part of the problem, of course, is that we probably put too much emphasis on individual high-profile cases, rather than more general patterns.…
