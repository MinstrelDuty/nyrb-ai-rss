# The Cat

October 30, 2026

[Read this issue](https://www.the-tls.com/issues/current-issue)

Along the wall at the cropped edge  
of vision comes the black four-legged  
creature picking her focused way  
over bricks laid sideways on

on their long edge until, like ink from a pen  
held at the wrong angle, she has drained  
completely away into the papery blank  
of near or far, who can tell.

Before she disappears inside the crop  
she stops, mid-stride, and turns her head  
with the late Queen’s blend  
of dutiful incuriosity and boredom, perhaps

resisting the urge to scratch, seeing  
none of the uses around her  
to which the baked earth has been put.  
Once there were crops on a riverbank

and from them she winnowed the mice  
so that her subjects might eat  
well and lie on their long edge at night  
and write and build into the sky.

_Will Eaves is an RLF Fellow at Brasenose College, Oxford. His most recent collection of poems is_ Invasion of the Polyhedrons _, 2024._
