# 正文

The eaglets nowhere to be  
seen, gone to make their  
own lives. The mother found

limping in a lake, injured, sick,  
attacked by two younger eagles.  
When do we become adults,

finally? After a mother dies?  
After a father dies? When  
your hands no longer look like

someone else’s? The dead hands  
are stuck in the earth’s drain,  
there’s a backup of fists in

a field. There was only one  
door, it turns out, but it opens to  
a cliff. No matter what I do,

I keep ageing out of life.  
Two small eaglets I watched  
for hundreds of hours, simply

vanished, not caring or  
wondering about the mother,  
now 170 miles away from their

nest, at a rehabilitation center –  
mother who huddled  
over them, within rain stanzas,

snow commas. If a mother is  
the form of a child, what  
happens when the form

is emptied? They say _nature_  
_is cruel_, but a child’s heart is  
crueller. They say these eaglets,

the same ones who knew  
their mother’s voice by heart,  
will no longer recognize her.

If forgetting is a symptom of  
survival, why does it hurt at  
the tip? My dead mother’s eyes

just appeared amongst the five  
trees outside, as the leaves  
grapple for a human face.

I no longer recognize her or  
see her face in my memories,  
memories that have no

fundament. Do I carry them now,  
heavy undercuts, or do I leave  
what’s left in the trees? Maybe the

dead must turn in their faces  
at a gate. If the mother eagle  
dies, the father will mate

with a new eagle, who will lay  
more eggs, this dazzling  
God spindle, that will turn into

new eaglets, who too will fledge in  
our hearts just as suddenly, like  
suicidal chariots. The children were

never mine to keep, just to assemble.  
I had them, held them like a  
sealed music box in my hands.

This year, I finally opened it,  
and the ballerina had vanished,  
but the music stayed.

_Victoria Chang’s most recent collection of poems,_ Tree of Knowledge _, 2026, is a finalist for the National Book awards. Her previous book,_ With My Back to the World _, 2024, won the Forward prize for best collection._
