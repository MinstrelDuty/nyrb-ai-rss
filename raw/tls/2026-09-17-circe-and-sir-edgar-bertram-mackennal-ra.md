# Circe and Sir Edgar Bertram Mackennal RA

*    
*    
*    
*    

![Image 1: The TLS front page from the issue dated Circe and Sir Edgar Bertram Mackennal RA. Issue number ](https://www.the-tls.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/circe-MB.jpg)

"Circe" by Bertram Mackennal, c.1902-4 | Art Gallery of New South Wales, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

I am about to go on a [tour of the UK](https://www.fane.co.uk/mary-beard?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=23483393028&gclid=CjwKCAjwtp7VBhBjEiwAJfpV--iz9GqkQRV5DCdgCxscgAf2WMZ7FTo1mHhuKgFLtJ9kGlS8BUYRBBoCV9YQAvD_BwE) and then [Australia and New Zealand](https://fane.com.au/whats-on/an-evening-with-mary-beard/), partly presenting my recent book _Talking Classics_, partly flying the flag for the ancient world more generally. When I go to different places, I always try to include images of local objects (a Roman soldier’s gravestone from Shrewsbury, for example, or the [Roman doctor’s kit](https://news.uq.edu.au/2016-02-25-ancient-medicines-and-bone-lever-rare-roman-medical-kit), now in Queensland). It’s polite to the audience and fun to find an ancient side near to wherever you are (not just the famous Greek and Roman sites). The search has also helped me to discover antiquities and works of art that I never knew about before – and occasionally to discover some great controversies.

For my new tour I have been tracking down modern images of [Circe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circe) (the magical witch, played by [Samantha Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Morton) in Christopher Nolan’s movie, who in Homer’s epic kept Odysseus as a sex slave for a year before she let him leave for home). She features in images everywhere. I was pleased to find, for example, the [wonderful painting of her from the 1920s](https://artuk.org/discover/artworks/circe-138546), with her animals, by [Maurice Greiffenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Greiffenhagen), now in the Hunterian in Glasgow (where I am going in October). But my absolute favourite discovery is a wonderful nineteenth-century bronze statue of Circe (at the top of this post), by the sculptor [Bertram Mackennal](https://www.artgallery.nsw.gov.au/collection/artists/mackennal-bertram/) (1863–1931), depicting her as a seductive temptress. I first came across it, in a small version, in the collection of [Birmingham Museum and Art Gallery](https://www.birminghammuseums.org.uk/birmingham-museum-and-art-gallery), which I will be putting on the screen when I go to Warwick in November.

To my shame, I had not heard of Mackennal. But when I looked for images of Circe in Australia, I found versions of this same statue everywhere – at small scale and as a full-sized bronze. And I soon discovered that he was a major player in art at the turn of the nineteenth and twentieth century, and that he was the first [Royal Academician](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/page/a-brief-history-of-the-ra) from Australia.
