# 正文

I tally the dead. I tally today’s dead. I seek a tally of the dead or seek  
yesterday’s dead. I try to count them, the dead. Reports tell me there are,  
today, more dead. I announce the dead—the house is quiet. I try to  
count. I seek numbers. I provide numbers. I wake trying. I cover  
myself in numbers, trying to keep still.

I seek some stillness beyond the dead—I try to just manage. I tally the dead,  
each pair of lungs, I wander through numbers, the daffodils nevertheless  
fly up. I hide the numbers, paper over what I know. The warm house fills.    
What are you doing, he says. I say prayers for, try to put faces to the dead

as if to anchor the dead—I condemn the dead, their utter defenselessness.    
Again I try to spare no numbers, the ones who have died, not the ones  
about to die, and when I wake they are there, counted and uncounted.    
When life takes more away, I keep the dead inside me, the ones not  
injured but gone, although many are injured. My counting—  
what even matters? I don’t feel like going out. Soon I am counting.

At the gate of the house I feel the faint dusk resolve. I bless the dead,  
I forget the dead from last year, I lose more dead, I tally what I can.    
I count through the morning. Light drifts across the thick rug. I move  
my hands as if I am counting the dead. As if I am altering the dead  
in my hands as I count.
