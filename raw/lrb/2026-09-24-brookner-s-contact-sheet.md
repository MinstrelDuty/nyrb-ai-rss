# 正文

![]()

My father, Peter Campbell, took these photographs of Anita Brookner for the *LRB* in 1982. The one at the bottom of the second column was used for the [cover of the 20 May issue](/the-paper/v04/n09). Brookner’s new novel, *Providence*, was reviewed by Robert Taubman along with four other novels, but his piece wasn’t advertised on the cover. The pictures must have been taken in Brookner’s office at the Courtauld: in a couple of them shelves of files are visible through the safety glass window in the door. I wonder if the Courtauld has any spaces as shabby as this any more. I suppose if I’d imagined her office at all it would have had bookshelves and framed prints, rather than bare lining paper, starting to come away from the wall, a sketch tacked up with drawing pins and mismatched desks stained with mug rings. It’s redolent of office life in the 1980s, but I thought the Courtauld would have been grander.

The photos in which Brookner has her back to the window are the least successful, being lit from behind, but they do show a couple of things. The radiator behind her suggests that the electric heater shown in the pictures taken with the light coming from the side was probably necessary: another illustration of early 1980s draughty bleakness. The typewriter looks like a Royal No. 10: even in 1982 quite antique, as they were only manufactured before 1940. Huge and very heavy, I think. This is why I’m fairly sure it was her office: it’s very much not a portable typewriter, and since there’s a typescript in the roller it’s clearly her own.

I’m guessing that Peter brought the tulips. Photographers like props because even the most interesting face can use help. He once pointed out to me that portrait photographers often like to have sitters’ hands in their pictures, often close to their faces, for the same reason. (I always think about that when I see pictures of stroppy Liam Gallagher, who keeps them behind his back, cussedly making the photographer’s work harder.)

My favourite is the top right, because it’s nice of Brookner and also has a bit more going on around the edges. But here Peter committed a photographer’s faux pas: his camera kit is clearly visible in the bottom left-hand corner. Not something you’d see David Bailey do.

As to how Peter came to take the picture at all, I’m guessing it was arranged at short notice and none of the *LRB*’s regulars – Chris Killip, Homer Sykes etc – was available. I think the only other portraits he did for the cover were of Ian McEwan and Ahdaf Soueif (the negatives of which have vanished). He did a sitting with V.S. Pritchett at home that didn’t provide anything very successful, which is probably the reason they weren’t used in the paper. But judging by Pritchett’s obvious cheerfulness in most of them, he and Peter were having a jolly time together.

Looking at all of the Brookner photos, the one that was chosen for the cover of the paper, and now for Hermione Lee’s biography, is very clearly the best of them. But neither the UK nor the US cover of Lee’s book is entirely successful. The US edition’s jacket copy obscures the tulips, breaking the composition, and the UK one reduces the image unnecessarily by shunting it off to the side.
